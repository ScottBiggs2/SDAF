Following "Generalization in diffusion models arises from geometry-adaptive harmonic representations", can we find these bases and tie them to interpretable concepts? This is an alternate and maybe novel alternative to the SAE monosemanticity concept, where instead of directions in $z$ we can identify bases, values, or even frequencies of these GAHBs with semantic concepts? A connected idea, we can track the vector direction very close to time $T$ to estimate the dimension of the true data manifold, as well as its Tangent and Normal spaces, which we could use to augment or more intelligently run our interp or steering experiments. 

We can check if the denoising field has interesting divergence/curl/laplacian at the boundaries of block latents. Since our VAE encodes blocks as $VAE(\theta_i, i) \rightarrow z_i$ and we then concatenate them as $\mathcal{Z} = \bigoplus_i^nz_i$, we should expect the flow to be strictly top-down (ie, higher blocks cannot depend on the results of lower blocks, since in the actual model we are mimicking they are computed from the top down.). It would be interesting to see if/how the diffusion model handles these boundaries. 

We can define a series of Borel $\sigma$-algebras on the generators, where each concept has a $\sigma$-algebra generated by sets of $\{\hat\theta_0,...,\hat\theta_T\}$ generation traces. This way we can get a natural heirarchy of these algebras with sub-$\sigma$-algebras. Since we can aso merge all of these algebras, we can do set differences between concept algebras. The idea would be to ultimately get this sophisticated enough that we could algebraically work backwards from an input to the actual input tokens used, or at least the input concepts at play. I would hazard to guess that the probability measure is well posed, since we are inside $\mathcal{Z}$, and can renormalize it to be a probability-measured space where directions should correspond roughly to concepts? We can also do filtrations across the timesteps in $\{\hat\theta_0,...,\hat\theta_T\}$ to see if we can pin down where in the trajectory concepts emerge or get decided.

### Overall verdict
- Ideas 1, 4 and 5 are sound and carry thesis chapters as written.
- Idea 2 needs a reframe. At optimum, a score field's Jacobian is symmetric, so "top-down" directionality *cannot* show up in the
  instantaneous field. It can show up in resolution order and in depth-ordered sampling.
- Idea 3 needs one fix. Real activations are (near-)injective in the input tokens, so plain σ-algebras collapse: everything is measurable.
  Make it non-trivial with **noise** (the t-filtration) or a **restricted function class** (V-information).
- Prior work you may be missing: crosscoders/transcoders (cross-layer SAE analogues), Kadkhodaie's two-disjoint-halves
  generalization protocol, diffusion intrinsic-dimension estimators (FLIPD), LM-inversion results, and Park et al. on hierarchical concepts.
  All are below. I know of no work that does score-geometry interp on *LLM activation traces*; the GLP line is the closest.

### 1. GAHBs → concepts; manifold dimension and tangent/normal spaces

**Sound, with three adjustments.**

- **What GAHBs are.** At a given noisy input, the denoiser acts locally like an adaptive linear shrinkage. Its Jacobian
  eigenvectors form a *local*, input-dependent basis; the eigenvalues ≈ 1 span the retained (tangent-like) subspace, and the
  eigenvalues ≈ 0 are shrunk away.
- **Contrast with SAEs.** An SAE dictionary is a *global*, sparse, axis-aligned frame. A GAHB is a *local moving frame*.
  So "GAHB vs SAE" is really "local frames vs global dictionary", and that's a clean framing for the thesis. To tie a local basis to a
  concept, aggregate over a concept set:
  - the average projector P̄_A = E_{x∈A}[U_k U_kᵀ];
  - the stability of the top eigenvectors within A (principal angles);
  - separation between concepts (principal angles between P̄_A and P̄_B).
- **"Frequencies" probably won't carry over.** The harmonic/oscillatory character in images comes from convolutional, translation-equivariant
  inductive bias plus image geometry. Our latent has no spatial axis. The only ordered axis is depth (12 layer tokens), so at most
  you can define a "depth frequency" via a 12-point DCT or graph Fourier transform over layers. Treat that as exploratory. The *basis/eigenvalue* part transfers cleanly.
- **Time convention.** Dimension and tangent estimation happen at the **low-noise (data) end**: t→0 in DDPM/EDM, t→1 in FM.
  Make sure "close to time T" means that end.
- **Latent-space thickening (important).** VAE latents carry posterior noise σ_q(z|x). Below that noise scale, the data look
  full-dimensional in Z, so dimension estimates *must be read as a function of noise level*: a multiscale dimension profile.
  **Train the diffusion model on μ (deterministic latents)**, not on sampled z. rev-7's `export_latents.py` already writes μ.
- **Push forward to activation space.** Tangent vectors in Z map to activation space via the decoder Jacobian J_D (push-forward only;
  no pullback metric needed).
- **The best experiment in this cluster: Kadkhodaie's generalization protocol.**
  - Train two generators on *disjoint* halves of the cache and sample both from the same noise.
  - If they produce near-identical traces, the model generalizes (it has learned the density). If not, it memorizes.
  - Sweep dataset size to find the memorization → generalization transition.
  - It's cheap and reviewer-proof, and it validates every later geometric claim.

**Prior work to read and cite:**
- Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli, Mallat (2310.02557). GAHB and the disjoint-halves protocol.
- Mohan, Kadkhodaie, Simoncelli, Fernandez-Granda, "Robust and Interpretable Blind Image Denoising via Bias-Free CNNs" (ICLR 2020).
  The origin of the denoiser-Jacobian-as-adaptive-basis analysis.
- Kadkhodaie & Simoncelli, "Solving Linear Inverse Problems Using the Prior Implicit in a Denoiser" (2007.13640).
- Stanczuk et al. (2212.12611). Normal space from the score's SVD.
- Kamkari et al., FLIPD, local intrinsic dimension from diffusion models (2406.03537). A cheaper LID estimator. (verify)
- **Positioning against SAEs:** Bricken 2023, Templeton 2024, Gao 2024 (in the study guide §8).

### 2. Divergence/curl at block boundaries; expected top-down structure

**Needs a reframe. The intuition about directionality is right, but it can't live in the instantaneous field.**

- **Joint distributions are symmetric in dependence.** Block 0 isn't *computed from* block 11, but the two are correlated.
  Denoising z₀ given a noisy z₁₁ legitimately uses z₁₁, the same way you'd infer an input from an output. Also, per token, z_{l+1} isn't a
  function of z_l alone (attention reads other positions), so even a Markov chain over depth doesn't hold.
- **The score's Jacobian is a Hessian, hence symmetric.**
  - s_t = ∇ log p_t, so ∂s_t/∂z = ∇² log p_t, and ∂s_l/∂z_m = (∂s_m/∂z_l)ᵀ exactly.
  - The same holds for the probability-flow ODE velocity, and for the FM marginal velocity with Gaussian paths (affine in x and the score).
  - Consequence: at optimum, cross-layer coupling is **symmetric**. "Curl" (the antisymmetric part (J − Jᵀ)/2; curl proper is 3D-only)
    measures **model error / non-conservativeness**, not GPT-2 structure. That's still worth reporting as a diagnostic.
  - Background: Salimans & Ho, "Should EBMs model the energy or the score?" (2021).
- **What *is* sound and interesting:**
  - **Coupling magnitude ‖∂s_l/∂z_m‖ over (t, l, m).** Expect strong near-diagonal banding (adjacent layers share most of the residual stream).
    Examine how the bandwidth changes with t.
  - **Divergence / Laplacian.** ∇·s = Δ log p_t. Its per-layer block trace gives "how concentrated is layer l's marginal at noise t".
    This is sound and cheap (Hutchinson estimator).
  - **Directionality, via depth-ordered generation (Diffusion Forcing, 2407.01392).**
    - Train with *independent per-layer noise levels*.
    - Sample in depth order (0→11), reverse depth order (11→0), and jointly.
    - Compare sample fidelity and weight-consistency.
    - If depth order (0→11) beats reverse, the generator finds GPT-2's computational direction easier to model. That's a *legitimate* directional claim and a strong figure.
  - **Resolution order under joint sampling** (from Round 3/4): which layers commit first as t advances.

### 3. σ-algebras on generation traces

**The core idea can be rescued and made sharp. Four technical fixes:**

1. **Injectivity collapses plain σ-algebras.**
   - Real activations are (essentially) an injective function of the input tokens, and z is a near-invertible compression of them.
     So σ(z) ≈ σ(tokens), and "work backwards to the input tokens" is achievable in principle.
   - Prior work does exactly that: vec2text (Morris et al., 2310.06633); language-model inversion from logits (Morris et al., 2311.13647);
     and a recent result that transformer LMs are injective with exact recovery of inputs from hidden states (Nikolaou et al., 2025 — verify).
   - So the novelty can't be invertibility. It has to be **which concepts are measurable under constraints.**
2. **Two ways to make measurability non-trivial:**
   - **Noise:** σ(x_t) for t > 0 (the filtration; see 4).
   - **A restricted function class:** "A is V-measurable" if a simple predictor (e.g. linear) of x recovers 1_A. Use **V-information**
     (Xu et al., "A Theory of Usable Information under Computational Constraints", 2002.10689), and information-theoretic probing
     (Pimentel et al., 2004.03061).
   - The linear case ties straight to the linear representation hypothesis: a concept is linearly represented iff its event lies (approximately) in
     the σ-algebra generated by one linear functional, i.e. half-spaces {z : w·z > b}.
3. **Operations.**
   - The union of two σ-algebras isn't a σ-algebra. Use the **join** σ(F ∪ G).
   - "Set difference of algebras" isn't standard. Use event differences (A \ B), or conditional information I(1_A ; z | F_B):
     what A adds beyond B.
   - **Hierarchy ↔ sub-σ-algebras:** σ(animal) ⊆ σ(species). Compare with Park et al., "The Geometry of Categorical and Hierarchical Concepts
     in LLMs" (2406.01506), who find hierarchical concepts as orthogonal directions/simplices. Contrast "directions" (theirs) with
     "measurable events under a function class" (ours).
4. **Which measure.**
   - The Borel σ-algebra on Z ⊂ ℝ^{12·d} is the natural one.
   - Two candidate probability measures:
     - the **data measure** (the pushforward of the corpus through GPT-2 → E): the scientifically meaningful one;
     - the **model measure** p_θ (the generator's pushforward of a Gaussian): the computable one.
   - Their mismatch is the generator's error. Report results under p_θ and validate on held-out data samples.
   - No "renormalization" is needed; both are already probability measures.
   - Note: *directions* aren't events. Concepts-as-events are sets (probe level sets), and directions enter only through the function class.

### 4. Filtrations over generation time

**Sound.** (Already developed in recap_0928, Round 3.) Two reminders:
- Define it through the forward-noising posterior p(x₀ | x_t), not the ODE path: under the probability-flow ODE the filtration is trivial.
- Measure commitment with calibrated noisy classifiers or V-information as a function of t, per layer.
- **Prior work it must be positioned against:** Biroli et al. (2402.18491), Li & Chen critical windows (2403.01633), Sclocchi et al.
  (2402.16991), Raya & Ambrogioni (2305.19693). Novelty = the *per-layer* commitment map on LLM traces, compared with GPT-2's depth order.

### 5. GLP comparison

**Essential, and well chosen.** To keep it fair:
- Train GLP on **GPT-2** (a single layer's residual, using their code) on the *same* corpus/positions, with roughly matched params and compute.
  Don't compare against their published Llama numbers.
- **Shared experiments:**
  - (a) Steering with on-manifold projection: the attribute-vs-fluency trade-off curve.
  - (b) Probing denoiser features.
  - (c) Sample fidelity at the shared layer.
- **Our-only experiment:** project a steered layer-l activation with GLP, run GPT-2 forward through layers l+1…11, and measure downstream
  divergence. Compare with our full-trace projection, which is cross-layer consistent by construction (scored with the weight-consistency metrics).
  This is the single most important figure for the "full trace" claim.
